# ⏱️ Time-Before-Goals Practice

> **Research-based planning rule for multi-platform video production**

## The Practice

When planning weekly video production across multiple platforms (YouTube Long, Shorts, LinkedIn, X), **budget your available time into discrete blocks FIRST**, then set realistic output goals for each block.

**Don't:** Start with targets ("draft 3 Shorts, 1 long-form, 5 LinkedIn posts") and try to fit them into your week.

**Do:** Start with time blocks ("Mon 2h writing / Tue 1h recording / Wed 1h editing") and set achievable targets for each block.

## Why It Works

### The Problem: Target-First Overestimates
Setting output targets first ("I'll create X slides, Y videos, Z posts in 2 hours") systematically **overestimates how much time each task actually needs**. You end up:
- Spreading yourself too thin across competing goals
- Underdelivering on multiple fronts
- Feeling dissatisfied even when you made progress

### The Solution: Time-First Calibrates
Allocating time blocks first ("60m for writing / 30m for research / 30m for editing") **disaggregates your total available time into mental accounts**, which leads to:
- Better-calibrated (often lower, more achievable) targets
- Better distribution of time and output across goals
- Higher overall progress and satisfaction

### The Mechanism
When you budget time first, you naturally ask "what can I realistically produce in 60 minutes?" instead of "how long will this big target take?" The first question anchors you to reality; the second invites wishful thinking.

## Research Citation

**Memmi, S. A., & Etkin, J.** (2023). Time before goals: The benefits of budgeting time first for multiple goal setting and pursuit. *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, *195*, 104148.

Key finding: Across 12 experiments, participants who budgeted time before setting targets showed better progress distribution, higher completion rates, and greater satisfaction compared to those who set targets first.

## Applied to Video Production Calendar

### Weekly Planning Workflow

#### ❌ Target-First (Overestimates)
"This week I'll create:
- 1 YouTube long-form video (10-15 min)
- 3 YouTube Shorts
- 2 LinkedIn posts with context
- 5 X threads
- 3 Claude Associate dictionary entries"

*Result: Overcommitted, likely to underdeliver, spreads time too thin*

#### ✅ Time-First (Calibrated)
"This week I have:
- **Monday 3h** → Research + script 1 YouTube long-form (Core Concepts)
- **Tuesday 1.5h** → Record + edit 1 Short (What is a Prompt?)
- **Wednesday 1h** → LinkedIn post with series announcement
- **Thursday 1.5h** → Record + edit 1 Short (Context Window)
- **Friday 1h** → LinkedIn post + X thread (study guide)
- **Saturday 1h** → Record + edit 1 Short (What is a Token?)"

*Result: Realistic scope per time block, better progress, higher satisfaction*

### Sprint 1 Example: Claude Associate Dictionary

Instead of "Ship 6 dictionary entries this sprint," start with:

**Available time blocks:**
- Week 1: 8 hours total (2h Mon, 2h Tue, 1h Wed, 2h Thu, 1h Fri)
- Week 2: 7 hours total (2h Mon, 1.5h Tue, 1h Wed, 1.5h Thu, 1h Sat)

**Then allocate:**
- Mon 2h → Core Concepts (research + outline)
- Tue 2h → Prompt definition (script + record)
- Wed 1h → Context Window (edit + publish)
- Thu 2h → Token explanation (script + record)
- Fri 1h → Review + schedule releases

**Output goals emerge from time reality**, not the other way around.

## Integration with OKRs

**Key Result 1**: Maintain weekly cadence across all platforms
- **Time-first approach**: Budget total weekly hours first (e.g., 15h/week), allocate to Mon/Tue/Thu/Sat slots, then set platform targets
- **Outcome**: Sustainable cadence without burnout

**Key Result 2**: Ship Sprint 1 Claude Associate dictionary
- **Time-first approach**: Count sprint hours (e.g., 30h total), allocate research/record/edit blocks, then decide how many entries fit
- **Outcome**: Realistic sprint scope, higher completion rate

## When to Use This Practice

**Always use when:**
- Planning multi-platform weekly production schedule
- Starting a new sprint with multiple deliverables
- Juggling YouTube Long + Shorts + LinkedIn + X simultaneously
- Time is the limiting constraint (it usually is)

**Can skip when:**
- Single-focus day (e.g., "today is 100% YouTube Long day")
- One clear priority with no competing goals
- Time is not the bottleneck (rare in video production)

## Quick Reference

> **When juggling multiple goals in limited time:**
>
> 1. **List available time blocks** (Mon 2h, Tue 1.5h, Wed 1h…)
> 2. **Allocate blocks to platforms/activities** (Mon → YT Long, Tue → Shorts…)
> 3. **Set realistic output goals per block** (Mon 2h → script + outline 1 video)
> 4. **Track and adjust** (if a block takes longer, reduce output goal, don't add hours)
>
> Result: Better progress distribution, higher satisfaction, sustainable cadence.
